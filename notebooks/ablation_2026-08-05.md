## Rejected variant: normalized full deployment (tested 2026-08-05)

Motivation: production sizing (weight = 1.5% vol budget / asset vol) left mean
gross exposure at 42%, so a meaningful share of return was RF on idle cash.
Tested replacing it with inverse-vol weights normalized to sum to 1.0.

| | Vol-budget (production) | Normalized (rejected) |
|---|---|---|
| Mean gross | 42% | 75% |
| Ann return | 4.37% | 4.37% |
| Ann vol | 3.89% | 4.73% |
| Excess Sharpe | 0.749 | 0.615 |
| Max DD | -5.4% | -14.4% |
| Calmar | 0.804 | 0.304 |
| Turnover | 277% | 507% |

Rejected. 78% more capital deployed produced identical return, ~3x the
drawdown, and ~2x the turnover.

Mechanism: normalization forces constant gross regardless of how many assets
pass the trend filter, so the book is MOST concentrated when the FEWEST assets
are trending - typically mid-selloff. Under vol-budget sizing gross shrinks
naturally as assets drop out; that shrinkage is the drawdown protection.
Renormalizing after every signal change also cascades trades across all
holdings whenever any single asset flips, explaining the turnover jump.

Note: the per-asset vol budget is NOT equivalent to the aggregate vol targeting
removed in Fix 2. The aggregate version levered UP in calm markets (harmful);
the per-asset budget only scales down and has no leverage mechanism.
